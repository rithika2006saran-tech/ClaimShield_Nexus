# Machine learning

All figures below were produced by executing `python -m app.pipeline` and are stored in `model_runs` (served at `GET /api/models`). They are **synthetic-data results**: labels come from injected scenarios, so they show the pipeline can recover those scenarios, not how it would perform on real claims.

## Isolation Forest - anomaly score
Provider-level behaviour features (`IF_FEATURES`: claims/month, avg allowed, paid/month, claims/member, unique members/facilities, duplicate rate, referral concentration in/out, degree, new connections, high-complexity ratio). `IsolationForest(n_estimators=300, contamination="auto")`. The **anomaly score** is the percentile rank (0-1) of `-score_samples`. It is *never* called a fraud probability; evidence text says so explicitly.

## XGBoost - primary supervised FWA risk model
- Unit: provider-month panel (rolling 3-month features, peer-relative ratios, duplicate/unbundling/implausible/timing counts, referral and connection growth; 18 features in `MODEL_FEATURES`).
- Label: provider-month with >=3 injected-FWA claims (synthetic ground truth, never a feature).
- **Chronological split:** train months 4-11 (n=7,692), validation 12-13 (n=1,992, early stopping + threshold), test 14-18 (n=4,995; 345 positives; base rate 6.9%).
- Params: `n_estimators<=400, max_depth=4, lr=0.05, subsample=0.85, colsample_bytree=0.85`, early stopping (best iteration 285).

| Model (test months 14-18) | PR-AUC | Precision | Recall | P@25 | P@50 | P@100 | R@100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **XGBoost** (validation-chosen threshold) | **0.875** | 0.875 | 0.710 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.287 |
| Rules-only baseline | 0.384 | 0.771 | 0.449 | 0.76 | 0.72 | 0.71 | 0.206 |
| Isolation Forest baseline | 0.663 | 0.754 | 0.409 | 1.00 | 0.96 | 0.93 | 0.270 |

R@100 is low by construction: only 100 of 345 positives can be recovered in the top 100.
**Caveat (circularity):** several features are counts of rule-style behaviours (duplicates, timing, unbundling) and the labels are generated by the same injected scenarios, so the metrics are optimistic about recoverability. They must not be quoted as real-world performance. The XGBoost output is called the **FWA risk score**, not a probability of fraud.

## SHAP
`shap.TreeExplainer` on the XGBoost model; per provider the top positive and negative contributors (log-odds) are stored and shown as `SHAP` evidence and in the Workspace/Provider views. SHAP explains the *model*; it is **not proof of misconduct**.

## 30/60/90 forecast
Separate XGBoost classifiers per horizon (label: >=3 injected-FWA claims in the next 30/60/90 days) followed by isotonic calibration. Chronological, purged split: train snapshots 4-8, calibration 9-11, purged 12-13, test >=14. A horizon passes only if **ECE <= 0.05, Brier < constant-baseline Brier, and >= 30 test positives**.

| Horizon | Test n / positives | Calibrated ECE | Calibrated Brier | Baseline Brier | Raw ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30-day | 3,995 / 280 | 0.013 | 0.033 | 0.066 | 0.039 |
| 60-day | 2,997 / 242 | 0.021 | 0.035 | 0.075 | 0.043 |
| 90-day | 1,998 / 169 | 0.022 | 0.036 | 0.078 | 0.047 |

All three passed, so outputs are labelled **probability** (synthetic). If any horizon fails, the pipeline automatically relabels all three **escalation index** (and the UI/Brief follow). Values are clipped to [0.01, 0.99] and made monotone across horizons. Note that extreme cases such as the hero saturate near 0.99: this reflects a strongly separable synthetic scenario, not certainty.
